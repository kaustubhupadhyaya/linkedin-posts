A performance review once told me I was the bottleneck on my own team. I didn't believe it — until I checked the numbers.

Every pull request over $50k in scope routed through me for "final sign-off." I'd built that habit early, when the team was three people and I genuinely knew every corner of the codebase.

Eighteen months later, the team was nine people. I was still the sign-off. My manager pulled the data: average time-to-merge on anything needing my approval was 2.3 days. Everything else: 4 hours.

I wasn't protecting quality. I was protecting my own sense of being necessary.

We rewrote the approval rule that week: two senior engineers, not me, sign off on anything under $200k in scope. I stayed on call for the rest.

Merge time dropped to six hours within a month. My own output didn't slow down — it went up, because I stopped context-switching into other people's decisions all day.

What's a review comment that stung because it was true, not because it was unfair?
